WEEKLY REPORT — Intersection Counter
Week of May 23–29, 2026

================================================================
HEADLINE: We discovered the tool had North/South and East/West
labeled backwards at our test intersection — which would have put
the wrong direction counts into the final report — fixed it, and
roughly halved the remaining counting error (about 40% down to
about 22%) on that intersection this week.
================================================================

TOP-LINE SUMMARY
The whole week was spent on one thing: making the automated vehicle
counts accurate enough to trust. We brought in a second AI assistant
to pressure-test our approach, then methodically hunted down what was
keeping us from matching the professional reference counts. The big
result was catching a calibration bug that had quietly been mislabeling
travel directions — a problem that affected both our accuracy
measurements and the report a traffic engineer would hand to a client.

WHAT GOT DONE, BY DAY

Tue–Wed (May 26–27): Research and planning. We set up a second AI
assistant to collaborate on the hardest remaining accuracy problems and
had the two AIs work through better methods together, then turned that
into a concrete plan. We also began improving how the tool follows each
vehicle through the intersection and classifies its turn — the core of
replacing GoodVision/Miovision with our own tool.

Thu (May 28): Calibration — the setup screen that tells the tool where
each road and approach sits in the camera view. We refined how the tool
learns each approach's position and direction from the video itself. A
large part of the day was the computer automatically re-processing video
to test these changes.

Fri (May 29): The breakthrough day. While cross-checking our counts
against the professional reference data, we found the directions were
labeled backwards (and the cross-street legs were swapped). Fixing that
corrected both our accuracy scores and what would appear in the Excel
report. We then improved the vehicle-following so it stops over-counting
cars on the busy road, recovered several turn movements it had been
missing, and got the tool running on the computer's graphics chip — about
4x faster — so future test runs take a work session instead of overnight.

TIME SPENT (approximate)
  Vehicle-counting accuracy (following cars, classifying turns):  ~16 hrs
  Calibration setup (incl. the direction-labeling fix):           ~6 hrs
  Research & planning the accuracy approach:                      ~3.5 hrs
  Speeding up processing (graphics chip):                         ~1.5 hrs
  (Much of the above was the computer processing video on its own.)

WHERE THE PROJECT STANDS
The tool runs end-to-end — it watches the video, follows vehicles,
classifies their movements, and produces an Excel count report — and
straight-through traffic counts are now solid. The remaining gap is on
a few hard turn movements at this particular camera (low-resolution,
sun-glare). We've narrowed the realistic next step to either a smarter
vehicle-following method or, where the camera simply can't see a turn
well, flagging those for a quick human check. Net: the engine is
working and accurate on the common movements; the last stretch is
squeezing accuracy on the difficult turns before it's ready for
unattended daily use.
