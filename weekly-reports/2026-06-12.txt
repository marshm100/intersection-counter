Weekly Report — Intersection Counter
Week of June 6 – June 12, 2026

HEADLINE: Net counting error at the Bluffview camera fell from 14.6% to 2.5%
against the Miovision reference — our worst camera became our best — and it held
at 7% on a separate afternoon sample we hadn't tuned on. Separately, we built and
proved a way to calibrate a new intersection using none of Miovision's data.

SUMMARY
The focus was accuracy and self-reliance. We commissioned a deep technical review
of whether our approach matches the best published methods (it does), then used
the findings to fix specific counting failures and to remove our last dependence
on the commercial tool we're replacing. By Friday the live five-camera corridor
on N Belt Line Rd was reading 7.2% / 8.8% / 2.5% / 8.4% / 7.7% net error per
camera versus Miovision, and we had shipped the screens and self-checks needed to
run a brand-new site end to end. (Accuracy is measured as net error per turning
movement at 30-minute granularity against Miovision's professional counts.)

WHAT GOT DONE, BY DAY

Monday — Camera tuning + finishing the multi-camera engine.
Cut the E Town East Blvd camera's error from 16.4% to 8.8% by giving each camera
its own detection/tracking settings (duplicate-box suppression at 0.85, a stricter
0.90 match threshold). Finished the multi-camera backend: a queue that caps how
many cameras process at once so the laptop doesn't choke, a full automated test
covering the whole chain (upload → process → de-duplicate across cameras →
Excel), and an auto-upgrade that migrates older projects to the new format. Also
fixed the review screen's "add a missed vehicle" tool and its trajectory overlay.
Submitted the week's work for code review.

Wednesday — Calibration research + a scaled trial run.
Revisited the hand-drawn lane-path approach to setup and ran a multi-hour trial
processing pass to see how the full pipeline behaves at study scale.

Thursday — Deep research, then the accuracy rebuild.
A multi-source review confirmed our method (matching each vehicle's path shape to
reference paths) is the same technique that won the field's main academic counting
benchmark, and that sub-5% fully-automated accuracy exceeds what anyone has
published — so the realistic target is strong automation plus a short human check.
Acting on that: we diagnosed that fast vehicles were being lost not by the
detector but at "track birth" (their detections never clear the confidence bar to
start a track) compounded by low frame-rate footage; fixed it with buffered-overlap
matching and a looser track start backed by an after-the-fact quality filter
(Bluffview: 14.6% → 2.5%). We then built a bank-of-reference-paths generator that
learns each movement from the site's OWN traffic instead of from Miovision data —
it matched or beat the Miovision-built version on 3 of 5 cameras and won outright
on the held-out afternoon test (4.9% vs 7.1%).

Friday — Calibration screen, self-checks, and the full study.
Shipped the lane-drawing screen inside calibration (an engineer sets up a site in
under an hour). Added automatic sanity checks — flow conservation between adjacent
intersections and reverse-movement balance — that, on our test corridor, flagged
exactly the camera and direction we already knew were weak. Added a spot-count tool
that compares a hand-counted window to the system with proper confidence intervals
(about 850 vehicles certify a +/-10% margin) feeding a ship / review / fail gate,
plus a written new-site procedure. Finally, added the per-interval (15-minute) TMC
sheet to the Excel export and kicked off the full 6-hour and 24-hour studies.

WHERE THE PROJECT STANDS
The accuracy work this week followed a five-step plan (diagnose → fix tracking →
replace the Miovision dependency → add self-checks → define the acceptance gate),
and all five steps shipped. The corridor's worst camera is now its best, and four
of five cameras sit under 9% error. Two cameras (Northwest Dr and Barnes Bridge)
still trail because a leg sits directly on a through path; the fix is an operator
drawing their lane corridors in the new screen — no code change needed. The full
6h/24h study is processing now.
