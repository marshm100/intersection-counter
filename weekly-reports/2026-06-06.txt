Weekly Report — Intersection Counter
Week of May 31 – June 6, 2026

HEADLINE: All five camera views at our test intersection now run start-to-finish
for the first time, and counting error on the two worst views was cut by roughly
two-thirds (from ~29% off to ~8% off the professional reference).

SUMMARY
This week was about accuracy and coverage. We got the tool working across every
camera angle at our test intersection — not just one — and then spent most of the
week closing the gap between our vehicle counts and the counts from the commercial
tool (Miovision) we're trying to replace. The result is a tool that now produces a
full intersection's worth of numbers and is meaningfully closer to "trustworthy
enough to hand to a traffic engineer."

WHAT GOT DONE, BY DAY

Monday — Getting every camera working.
The intersection is filmed from five different angles, and until now the tool was
hand-tuned for just one or two. We rebuilt the processing so it can be pointed at
any camera with a single setting, then ran the remaining cameras overnight. By end
of day all five were producing counts — a real milestone, since a finished tool has
to handle the whole intersection, not a single approach. We also confirmed a smarter
way of telling vehicles apart frame-to-frame, which fixed a long-standing
over-counting problem on through-movements.

Tuesday — Counting accuracy, part one.
We tackled a recurring error where the tool would wrongly "snap" turning vehicles
onto the wrong path, inflating some movements. Fixing it dropped the error on two
camera views dramatically — one from ~23% to ~8%, and another from ~29% to under
10% across two stacked corrections the same day. We also
kicked off an unattended overnight test comparing several detector settings to see
which produced the cleanest counts.

Wednesday — Counting accuracy, part two.
We recovered turning movements the tool had been quietly dropping (one right-turn
went from almost none detected to about 40% of the true count — partial, but a real
improvement on a movement that was previously invisible) and added a guard so the corrections
don't overreach and create new errors. Net effect: steady, incremental accuracy
gains across the harder camera angles.

Thursday — A new calibration idea.
We prototyped a screen where the engineer can draw the actual lane paths through the
intersection by hand. This matters because our best current method leans on having
reference data we won't have at brand-new intersections — letting the engineer sketch
the lanes is a way to set up a new site from scratch. Early testing was promising.

WHERE THE PROJECT STANDS
The core engine — process video, track vehicles, classify turns, produce counts —
is working end-to-end across a full intersection. The active work now is accuracy
tuning: we're steadily driving our counts toward the commercial tool's, with the
worst views down to single-digit error. The main work ahead is closing that last
gap and making setup easy enough to use at intersections we've never seen before.

(Covers June 1–4; no work was logged on May 31, June 5, or June 6.)
