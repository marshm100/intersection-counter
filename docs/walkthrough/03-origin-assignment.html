3. Origin Assignment

Every track must be assigned an origin leg before its movement can be classified. The pipeline tries three independent signals in order of specificity, taking the first that succeeds.

The three tiers

Tier 1: Tripwire crossing

Walk every consecutive pair of trajectory points. For each leg, ask did_cross_line(p[i-1], p[i], tripwire_start, tripwire_end) — a parametric segment-segment intersection. If it crossed, check direction via crossing_direction: the vehicle's motion vector must be within 90° of the leg's reference heading (i.e., it's entering, not exiting via the tripwire on its way back out).

The L19 / L20 failure mode (Bug A)

Vehicles entering NB Belt Line (L19) come from the southwest, off-frame. YOLO first detects them well inside the frame, already past the tripwire. Even with the tripwire extended to 120 px, the line ends at roughly (143, 475) while many vehicles are first detected at x ≈ 180-220 — beyond it.

Tier 2: Backward extrapolation

Added in the 2026-05-20 session. Takes the trajectory's first detection point and its early motion vector (smallest window that hits 15 px of displacement, capped at 8 frames). Projects that motion backward until it hits the frame boundary — that's where the vehicle would have entered the frame if YOLO had caught it earlier. Then assigns the leg whose origin point is nearest that synthetic entry, within 180 px.

Tier 3: Heading fallback

Last resort. Uses overall first→last displacement direction. For each leg, computes the angular difference between motion heading and the leg's reference heading; picks the closest leg, provided that difference is ≤ 90°.

Heading alone is ambiguous when two legs sit close in angle. At Sunnyvale, L18 (ref 259°) and L20 (ref 290°) are both westbound — a vehicle moving due west (heading 270°) satisfies the ≤90° enter check for both: diff to L18 = 11°, diff to L20 = 21°. The closer-by-angle leg wins regardless of where the vehicle actually came from.

This is exactly why Tier 2 runs BEFORE Tier 3 — backward extrapolation breaks the tie by position (where on the frame the vehicle came from), which heading alone can't see.
